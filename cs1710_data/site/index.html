Main message

Recent graduates should compare cities by how much they can save, not just what they earn.

A bigger paycheck does not mean more money left over

Starting salary tracks the cost of a modest budget closely, but it barely tracks what is left after that budget. The Midwest is where an entry salary goes furthest, not because pay is higher, but because rent takes about 20% of take-home instead of 32–45%.

About the data. One combined dataset for 40 U.S. metro areas (matched on CBSA code). Cost of living from the Economic Policy Institute Family Budget Calculator (single adult, 2026 edition, 2025 dollars). Jobs and wages from BLS OEWS (May 2025), keeping the 132 occupations that typically require a bachelor's degree and no experience. Population, rent and commutes from the Census ACS 2024. Price levels from BEA Regional Price Parities. Unemployment from BLS LAUS (2025). "Entry-level salary" is the employment-weighted 25th-percentile wage across those occupations. Savings = estimated take-home at that salary minus EPI's budget excluding taxes; the budget assumes car ownership everywhere, so transit-heavy metros such as New York are likely understated.
